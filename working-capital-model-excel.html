Working Capital Optimisation Model

Project and improve working capital for five business segments over 60 months: receivables with aging and bad debt, inventory by stage, payables, the cash conversion cycle, seasonal financing need, three financing options and the cash released by shorter collection or longer payment terms.

The problem it solves

Working capital is where profitable businesses run short of cash. Receivables stretch, inventory builds ahead of the season, and finance is asked how much cash a ten-day improvement in collections would free up, and whether factoring would cost less than the overdraft.

This model answers both. It builds receivables, inventory and payables from days assumptions for each segment, prices each improvement in cash released and interest saved, and compares the overdraft, factoring and supply chain finance on cost.

Who it's for

CFOs, treasurers and FP&A teams at companies carrying receivables and inventory. Working capital and transformation consultants. Lenders and analysts assessing a borrower's cash cycle. Students learning the cash conversion cycle.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Scenario selector and multipliers, base rate, tax and WACC, and for each of five segments: revenue, growth, COGS and purchases ratios, target DSO, DIO and DPO, provision rates, an aging profile and monthly seasonal factors.

RECEIVABLES

Gross and net receivables, bad debt provision, five aging buckets, DSO, collection efficiency and overdue share by segment.

INVENTORY

Raw materials, work in progress and finished goods, obsolescence provision, DIO, turnover and carrying cost by segment.

PAYABLES

Trade payables, accruals and other payables, DPO, early payment discount and payables aging by segment.

NWC_SUMMARY

Net working capital, change in NWC, NWC days and financing need by segment and consolidated.

CASH_CONVERSION

DSO, DIO, DPO, cash conversion cycle, cash tied up and its financing cost by segment and blended.

SEASONAL

Seasonal revenue and NWC by month, peak and trough flags, and the seasonal financing need or surplus.

FINANCING

Overdraft, receivables factoring and supply chain finance compared on monthly cost.

OPTIMISATION

Cash release, new NWC and interest saving for 5, 10 and 15-day improvements in DSO, DIO and DPO, and a combined case.

AGING_DETAIL

Receivables aging and expected loss by customer tier, payables by supplier category, and concentration measures including a Herfindahl index.

DASHBOARD

KPIs and six charts.

SENSITIVITY

NWC days by DSO and DPO change, cash impact by revenue growth and NWC days, and CCC by DSO and DIO change.

CHECKS

64 integrity checks, including NWC equal to receivables plus inventory less payables, and CCC equal to DSO plus DIO less DPO, for every segment.

What makes it different

Days turned into cash

Every improvement is priced: a 5, 10 or 15-day change in DSO, DIO or DPO shows the cash released, the new NWC and the interest saved.

Five segments, one consolidated view

Manufacturing, retail, technology, services and healthcare segments, each with its own drivers, roll up to blended DSO, DIO, DPO and CCC.

Seasonality made visible

Monthly seasonal factors drive revenue, so the model flags the peak and trough working capital months and the financing the peak needs.

Financing options compared

Overdraft, factoring and supply chain finance costed side by side against the same financing need.

Aging and concentration

Five aging buckets with loss rates that rise with age, customer tiers, supplier categories and a Herfindahl concentration index.

Frequently asked questions

What does it not do?

Inventory is split 40% raw materials, 25% work in progress and 35% finished goods, accruals and other payables are set as shares of COGS, and factoring and supply chain finance use fixed eligibility and advance rates. Days are calculated on a 30-day month, and interest saving is cash released times the base rate.

What do the scenarios change?

Base, Upside, Downside and Stress adjust revenue, costs, DSO and DPO together. In the demo, Stress takes revenue down 30%, costs up 15%, DSO out 10 days and DPO in 10 days.

Which currency is it in?

The demo is in US dollars. All values are inputs, so any currency works if you are consistent.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample shows every tab, chart and check with real results on the demo company, plus one engine tab with live formulas.
