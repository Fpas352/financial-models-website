Budget & Forecast Model

Build a full-year budget for ten departments month by month: revenue across eight streams, operating costs across 20 categories, headcount with hires and leavers, capex and depreciation, then a consolidated P&L, a monthly cash budget and a departmental comparison, under four scenarios from one selector.

The problem it solves

Budget season means pulling revenue, cost, headcount and capex plans from every department into one P&L, then redoing it when the growth or inflation assumption moves. The consolidation is where errors creep in: a department missing from a total, a quarter that does not add to the year.

This model builds the budget from department assumptions and consolidates it automatically. Revenue growth, cost and salary inflation, headcount and capex each carry Base, Upside, Downside and Stress values, and one selector moves the whole budget between them.

Who it's for

FP&A teams and budget owners preparing the annual budget. Finance directors who need the P&L, cash and headcount plans to agree. Analysts and students learning how a departmental budget consolidates.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Scenario selector, growth, inflation, tax, bonus, benefits and working capital assumptions, annual revenue by department and stream, headcount and salary per head by department, and capex by category with useful lives.

REVENUE_BUDGET

Monthly revenue by department and stream, with quarters and full year.

COST_BUDGET

Monthly operating costs by department across 20 categories.

HEADCOUNT

Opening headcount, hires, leavers, closing and average headcount, salary, bonus and benefits by department.

CAPEX_PLAN

Monthly capex across six asset categories, straight-line depreciation by useful life, and net book value.

PL_BUDGET

Consolidated P&L from revenue to net income, monthly with quarters and full year.

CASH_BUDGET

Operating cash flow with receivable and payable movements from days assumptions, capex, financing and the closing cash balance.

DEPARTMENTAL

P&L by department to EBIT, contribution margin, revenue per FTE, and monthly revenue, cost and contribution by department.

PHASING

Each department's monthly revenue and cost as a share of the year, and cumulative phasing.

VARIANCE

Budget against prior year by department and month. The prior-year rows are a placeholder set at 95% of budget: replace them with your actuals.

DASHBOARD

Chart data for revenue by department, cost structure, cash flow, headcount, margins and capex.

SENSITIVITY

EBITDA by revenue growth and cost inflation, and people cost by headcount change and salary inflation.

CHECKS

47 integrity checks, including monthly P&L revenue agreeing to the revenue budget, quarters summing to the year, and the headcount roll-forward by department.

What makes it different

Four scenarios, one selector

Growth, inflation, headcount, salary, bonus and capex assumptions each carry Base, Upside, Downside and Stress values. Change the selector and every department, the P&L and the cash budget move together.

Headcount that rolls forward

Opening headcount plus hires less leavers gives closing headcount every month, and people cost follows from salary per head, bonus and benefits.

Capex with its depreciation

Six asset categories, each depreciated straight-line over its own useful life, feeding D&A in the P&L and capex in the cash budget.

Cash, not just profit

Receivable and payable movements from days assumptions turn the P&L into a monthly cash budget with a closing balance.

Totals that are checked

Every month of P&L revenue is checked against the revenue budget, and quarters against the year.

Frequently asked questions

What does it not do?

It is a single-year monthly budget. Cost of goods sold is set at 30% of revenue, revenue and costs are spread evenly across the months, interest is charged on a fixed debt balance, and the prior-year comparison is a placeholder until you enter last year's figures.

Does it compare budget with forecast?

It builds the budget and the forecast cash position behind it. To track actuals against the budget through the year, see the Budget vs Actual Variance Model; to keep an 18-month forward view, see the Rolling Forecast Model.

Is it built for banks?

The demo departments are a bank's, from Retail Banking to Human Resources, but the structure is generic: departments, revenue streams, cost categories, headcount and capex.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample shows every tab, chart and check with real results on the demo bank, plus one engine tab with live formulas.
