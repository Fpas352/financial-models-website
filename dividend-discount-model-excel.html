Dividend Discount Model (DDM)

Value dividend-paying companies five ways at once: Gordon growth, two-stage, H-model, free cash flow to equity and residual income, across a peer group of ten, with payout and yield analysis and a summary against the market price.

The problem it solves

For banks and other financial institutions, enterprise value and EBITDA mean little, so equity is valued from what shareholders receive. But a single-stage DDM is very sensitive to the growth assumption, and one method alone rarely convinces an investment committee.

This model runs four DDM variants and residual income side by side for ten companies, so you can see where the methods agree, where they diverge and why.

Who it's for

Equity analysts covering banks, insurers and other dividend payers. Investors screening income stocks. Students and candidates preparing for bank equity research and valuation interviews.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Per-company assumptions and cost of equity from CAPM, with Base, Upside, Downside and Stress scenarios.

DIVIDEND_PROJ

Twenty-year dividend projections for all ten companies.

GORDON_GROWTH

Single-stage value = DPS1 / (ke - g).

TWO_STAGE

A high-growth period followed by a Gordon terminal value.

H_MODEL

Growth declining linearly from the high rate to the stable rate.

FCFE_MODEL

Equity valued on cash available to shareholders, not just dividends paid.

RESIDUAL_INCOME

Book value plus the present value of returns above the cost of equity.

PAYOUT_ANALYSIS

Payout ratios moving towards a steady state.

YIELD_ANALYSIS

Dividend yield and dividend quality, including the Chowder number.

VALUATION_SUMMARY

All methods compared, with the premium or discount to market price.

DASHBOARD

Charts and visual summaries.

SENSITIVITY

Two-way tables for the main value drivers.

CHECKS

80 integrity checks.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use it outside banking?

Yes. Company names and every assumption are inputs. The notes flag where bank-specific features, such as residual income persisting in perpetuity, need adjusting for other sectors.

How is the cost of equity set?

CAPM: risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium, per company.

How long is the projection?

Twenty years of dividends, with growth transitioning between stages.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample shows every tab, chart and check with real results on the demo peer group, plus one engine tab with live formulas.
