The problem it solves

Every savings rate decision trades margin against balances, and a rate rise on one product pulls money from another. The question at pricing committee is rarely the cost of one product; it is the net effect on NII across the range.

This model puts that trade-off in one place: beta and lag by product, front book against back book, product profitability after FTP, and a pricing optimisation tab that shows cannibalisation.

Who it's for

Savings and deposit pricing teams. Treasury and ALM teams forecasting NII. Finance business partners supporting pricing committee and ALCO.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Bank Rate forward curve, opening balances, growth, betas, lags, spreads and floors per product, with Base, Upside, Downside and Stress scenarios.

BALANCES

60-month balances: inflows, withdrawals, maturities and rollovers for fixed-term products.

RATE_ENGINE

Effective rate by product = MAX(floor, Bank Rate x beta - spread), with lag, and front book against back book.

NII_CALC

Interest expense, income on deployed funds, NII and NIM.

PROFITABILITY

FTP credit, interest expense and direct costs by product, ranked by margin per million and cost per account.

PRICING_OPT

Rate optimisation and cannibalisation analysis.

REPRICING

Repricing gap and NII at risk.

DASHBOARD

Seven charts.

SENSITIVITY

NII impact of rate and beta changes.

CHECKS

37 integrity checks.

What makes it different

Beta, lag and floor by product

Each product passes on rate changes at its own speed and to its own extent, with a floor so rates never go below what you would pay.

Front book against back book

New business and existing balances priced separately, so you can see the cost of the rate differential.

Cannibalisation in the pricing test

PRICING_OPT shows the flows between products when one rate moves, so the net NII effect is visible.

Product economics after FTP

Net contribution per product after the FTP credit and direct costs, ranked.

NII at risk

A repricing gap and the NII effect of parallel rate shifts.

Frequently asked questions

How is it different from the Deposits Model?

The Deposits Model covers the whole deposit book including behavioural maturity and liquidity value. This model goes deeper on pricing: front and back book, optimisation, cannibalisation and NII at risk.

What does it not include?

Regulatory costs such as the FSCS levy are not in the pricing, market share is static rather than linked to competitor rates, and NII sensitivity assumes parallel curve shifts.

Which day count does it use?

Actual/365.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample is the complete workbook with every formula and check live, inputs locked, so you evaluate it on the demo bank.
