Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) Valuation Model

Value a group one division at a time. Eight segments, each with its own ten-year DCF, comparable multiples and precedent transactions, blended into a group value, then compared with the market to measure the conglomerate discount.

The problem it solves

A diversified group rarely trades at the sum of its parts. Analysts, activists and boards all ask the same question: what is each business worth on its own, and how large is the discount the market applies to the whole?

This model answers it segment by segment, with three methods per segment so no single assumption carries the answer, and benchmarks the implied discount against peers.

Who it's for

Equity research analysts covering diversified groups. Corporate development and strategy teams assessing disposals or spin-offs. Bank and financial services analysts valuing multi-line institutions.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Segment revenue, margins, growth, WACC and multiples, with Base, Upside, Downside and Stress scenarios.

SEGMENT_1 to SEGMENT_8

Eight segments, each with a ten-year P&L, free cash flow build and DCF. The demo is set up as a diversified financial group: consumer, corporate and investment banking, wealth and asset management, insurance, treasury and markets, and digital.

COMP_MULTIPLES

Comparable company and precedent transaction valuations per segment.

SOTP_SUMMARY

Blended enterprise value per segment and in total, and equity value per share.

CONGLOMERATE

Implied conglomerate discount against peer groups.

FOOTBALL_FIELD

Valuation ranges across all methods.

DASHBOARD

Six charts for board presentations.

SENSITIVITY

Two-way tables for the main value drivers, plus extended sensitivities.

CHECKS

63 integrity checks.

What makes it different

Three methods per segment

DCF, comparable companies and precedent transactions for every segment, blended 50 / 30 / 20 by default, with the weights as inputs.

A real DCF per segment

Ten-year explicit forecast, mid-year discounting, segment-specific WACC from CAPM and a Gordon growth terminal value capped below WACC.

The conglomerate discount, measured

The gap between the sum of the parts and the group's market value, benchmarked against peer conglomerates.

A proper equity bridge

Enterprise value less net debt, minorities and pension deficit, plus associates.

Board-ready output

A football field and six charts showing where each segment's value comes from.

Frequently asked questions

Is it only for banks?

No. The demo segments are a diversified financial group, but every segment's name and assumptions are inputs, so it works for any multi-division business.

Can I use fewer than eight segments?

Yes. Set unused segments to zero and they drop out of the total.

What does it not capture?

Optionality and synergy values are not modelled, and terminal value usually makes up a large share of each segment's DCF, as with any DCF.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample shows every tab, chart and check with real results on the demo group, plus one engine tab with live formulas.
