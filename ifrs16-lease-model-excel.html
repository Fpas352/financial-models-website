IFRS 16 Lease Model

Measure up to ten leases at commencement and run each one month by month for ten years: interest unwind, payments and depreciation. Rolled up into the P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, IFRS 16 disclosures and journals.

The problem it solves

Most IFRS 16 templates calculate one lease. Year-end needs the whole portfolio: a maturity analysis, right-of-use assets by class, the journals, and an answer on the two judgements auditors challenge most, extension options and the incremental borrowing rate.

This model runs the portfolio in one workbook and puts both judgements on a scenario selector, so you can show what each one does to the balance sheet.

Who it's for

Financial controllers and group reporting teams with a set of property, vehicle and IT leases. Bank finance teams accounting for a branch estate. Accountants preparing year-end files for clients.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Scenario, model start date, reporting year and the two judgements.

LEASES

Lease register: commencement, term, extension option, monthly payment, in advance or arrears, fixed annual uplift, incremental borrowing rate, initial direct costs, incentives and restoration costs. Measurement at commencement.

SCHEDULE

The monthly engine: payments, liability, interest, depreciation and right-of-use asset.

ANNUAL

Ten-year P&L, balance sheet, cash flow and roll-forwards.

DISCLOSURE

Maturity analysis and right-of-use assets by asset class.

JOURNALS

Initial recognition and reporting-year journal entries.

SENSITIVITY

Lease liability under shifted discount rates.

OUTPUTS

One-page summary and charts.

CHECKS

20 integrity checks, including that every lease ends within the horizon.

What makes it different

A portfolio, not one lease

Ten leases across property, vehicles, IT and equipment, each with its own dates, payments and rate, summed into one set of accounts.

The details that move the numbers

Payments in advance or in arrears, fixed annual uplifts, initial direct costs, lease incentives and restoration costs all feed the right-of-use asset at commencement.

Disclosures and journals included

The maturity analysis, the right-of-use table by class and the journal entries come from the same schedule, so they tie without rework.

The two judgements on a switch

Flex whether extension options are reasonably certain and the incremental borrowing rate, and see the effect on liability, asset and P&L.

Existing leases handled

For a lease already running at the model start date, enter the start date and the remaining term: the modified retrospective measurement.

Frequently asked questions

Lessee or lessor?

Lessee accounting only.

How many leases?

Ten rows, each a separate lease. For a larger estate, run a copy per entity or group leases of the same class and terms.

Does it handle index-linked payments?

Payments linked to an index are measured at the commencement rate, as IFRS 16 requires, so only fixed uplifts are projected. Remeasurements and modifications are not modelled.

Monthly or annual?

Monthly. A lease that commences mid-month is treated as starting on the first of that month. Every lease must end within the 120-month horizon; CHECKS tests this.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample is the complete workbook with every formula and check live, inputs locked, so you evaluate it on the demo portfolio.
