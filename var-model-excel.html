Value at Risk (VaR) Model

Value at Risk three ways on one portfolio: parametric, historical simulation and Monte Carlo. With expected shortfall, component and marginal VaR, stressed VaR, limits and a 250-day backtest scored on the Basel traffic light.

The problem it solves

A single VaR number hides its own assumptions. Parametric VaR assumes normal returns, historical simulation is only as good as its window, and Monte Carlo inherits whatever you feed it. Risk committees and auditors increasingly ask to see the methods side by side and to know whether the model has been right.

This model calculates all three on the same positions, decomposes the result by asset, stresses it, and backtests it against 250 days of P&L with the Basel traffic light and the Kupiec test.

Who it's for

Market risk and treasury risk teams. Finance professionals who need to explain VaR to a risk committee. Students and analysts learning how the three methods differ on real calculations.

What makes it different

Three methods, one portfolio

Parametric, historical and Monte Carlo VaR on the same positions, so the gap between them is visible and explainable.

Expected shortfall alongside VaR

The average loss beyond VaR, calculated from the historical distribution: the measure FRTB moved the trading book towards.

Where the risk comes from

Component VaR and marginal VaR by asset, with the diversification benefit shown explicitly.

Stress that removes diversification

Stressed VaR multiplies volatilities and pushes correlations towards one, which is what happens in a real crisis.

Backtested the regulatory way

Exceptions over 250 days scored on the Basel traffic light (green up to 4, amber 5 to 9, red 10 or more) and tested with the Kupiec proportion-of-failures test.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my own positions and data?

Yes. Positions, holding period, confidence level and the market data window are inputs. Replace the 250 days of prices on MARKET_DATA with your own series.

Does it handle options?

No. It is a linear VaR model, so it does not capture option gamma or convexity. Multi-day VaR uses the square-root-of-time rule.

How many simulations does the Monte Carlo use?

250 scenarios drawn from the estimated distribution, which keeps it fast in Excel. It shows the method clearly; production Monte Carlo engines run many more paths.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample is the complete workbook with every formula and check live, inputs locked, so you evaluate it on the demo portfolio.
