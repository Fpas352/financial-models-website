Budget vs Actual Variance Model

Monthly budget against actual for eight departments and 40 revenue and cost lines each: variance in pounds and percent, a consolidated P&L, year-to-date and run-rate views, prior-year comparison, trends and an EBITDA bridge from budget to actual.

The problem it solves

Month-end variance reporting is mostly mechanics: line up budget and actual, work out which way is favourable for revenue and for cost, total it by department and explain the bridge. Done by hand each month, the sign conventions slip and the totals stop tying.

This model fixes the mechanics once. Enter actuals on one tab and every variance, in pounds and percent, is signed so that positive always means favourable, rolled up by department and group, and bridged from budget EBITDA to actual.

Who it's for

FP&A teams and management accountants running monthly variance reporting. Department heads and finance business partners who need a line-by-line view. Finance students learning how a variance pack is built.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Fiscal year, the monthly budget and the prior-year figures for every line in every department.

BUDGET

Approved budget by department and line, monthly, with year to date and full year.

ACTUALS

Where actual results are entered, in the same layout as the budget.

VARIANCE_ABS

Variance in pounds, signed so positive is favourable for both revenue and cost.

VARIANCE_PCT

The same variances as a percentage of budget.

PRIOR_YEAR

Actual against prior year for every line.

CONSOLIDATED

Group P&L across all departments: budget, actual, variance and variance percent by month.

YTD_ANALYSIS

Year-to-date budget, actual and variance, full-year budget, a run-rate full-year projection and completion percentage.

TREND

Twelve-month trends by department with 3-month rolling averages and a seasonality index.

WATERFALL

EBITDA bridge from group budget to actual through each department's revenue and cost variance, and a department EBITDA comparison.

DASHBOARD

Six charts.

SENSITIVITY

Group EBITDA under revenue and cost changes by department, and a two-way revenue by cost grid.

CHECKS

70 integrity checks, including department totals tying to the budget and EBITDA equal to revenue less costs in both budget and actuals.

What makes it different

One sign convention

Positive always means favourable: revenue above budget or cost below it. No mental flipping between revenue and cost lines.

Line-level detail, group-level answer

Eight departments with 15 revenue and 25 cost lines each roll up to a consolidated P&L and a group EBITDA.

The bridge, built for you

WATERFALL walks budget EBITDA to actual through each department's revenue and cost variance.

Year to date and full year

YTD variance, full-year budget and a run-rate projection show where the year is heading, not just where the month landed.

Prior year as well as budget

Every line is compared with last year as well as with plan.

Frequently asked questions

What does it not do?

Actuals are entered by hand on ACTUALS; there is no ledger import. It covers one fiscal year of 12 months, and the run-rate projection annualises year-to-date actuals by the current calendar month, so it assumes a January to December year.

Can I use it outside banking?

Yes. The demo is a bank with departments such as Retail Banking and Treasury, but the structure is departments and line items. Rename them to match your own chart of accounts.

How is it different from the Budget & Forecast Model?

That model builds the annual budget from revenue, cost, headcount and capex assumptions. This one takes a budget as given and tracks actual results against it every month.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample is the complete workbook with every formula and check live, inputs locked, so you evaluate it on the demo bank.
