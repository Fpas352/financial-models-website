Capitalisation & Intangible Assets Model (IAS 38)

Test development spend against the six IAS 38 criteria, carry 120 intangible assets across eight categories, and roll cost, amortisation, impairment and net book value forward month by month for five years, ending in the year-by-year reconciliation for the accounts.

The problem it solves

Whether development spend can be capitalised is a judgement auditors challenge every year, and the evidence for it often sits in emails and a spreadsheet that one person understands.

This model puts the assessment on one tab: each project is scored Yes or No against the six criteria, from technical feasibility to reliable measurement, and passes when all six are met. Research spend is expensed, development spend is split between capitalised and expensed at a rate you set, and the schedules roll cost, amortisation and impairment forward to a closing net book value.

Who it's for

Technical accounting teams and financial controllers at companies with material software, development or acquired intangibles. Finance teams preparing the intangible assets note for audit. Auditors and advisers who want a transparent benchmark for a client's capitalisation policy.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Start date, 60-month horizon, impairment discount rate, tax rate and capitalisation threshold; then useful life, method, residual value, additions, disposals and impairment trigger for each of eight categories; plus R&D spend, research share and the development capitalisation rate. Base, Upside, Downside and Stress scenarios.

CAPITALISATION_TEST

40 projects scored against the six IAS 38 criteria: technical feasibility, intention to complete, ability to use or sell, probable future economic benefits, adequate resources and reliable measurement. PASS needs all six.

ASSET_REGISTER

120 assets, 15 in each category, with cost, useful life, method, accumulated amortisation, net book value and status: active, near end or fully amortised.

COST_ROLL

Monthly gross cost by category: opening, additions, reclassifications, disposals and closing.

AMORTISATION

Monthly charge by category on cost less residual value over useful life, with amortisation released on disposals. Goodwill is not amortised.

NBV_SCHEDULE

Gross cost less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment, by category and month, with NBV as a percentage of cost.

RD_ANALYSIS

Total R&D spend split into research (expensed), development capitalised and development expensed, with the capitalisation rate and cumulative totals.

IMPAIRMENT

Annual test by category: carrying amount against recoverable amount, the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use.

PL_IMPACT

Amortisation, impairment and R&D expense through to EBIT, monthly and as an annual summary.

DISCLOSURE

Reconciliation of opening to closing NBV by category for each of the five years, plus a table of methods, useful lives, residual values and rates.

DASHBOARD

KPIs and chart data: NBV by category, amortisation trend, R&D capitalisation, asset age profile and an NBV waterfall.

SENSITIVITY

Useful life moved by up to three years either way, the R&D capitalisation rate flexed, and a two-way grid of useful life against impairment trigger.

CHECKS

53 integrity checks, including that goodwill amortisation is zero and that each category's NBV equals cost less amortisation and impairment.

What makes it different

The six criteria, documented

Each project is assessed criterion by criterion, so the file shows why an item was capitalised, not just that it was.

Research and development separated

Research spend goes to the P&L. Development spend is split between capitalised and expensed at a rate you set, and the split is visible every month.

Goodwill treated as goodwill

An indefinite life, no amortisation, an annual impairment test, and a check on CHECKS that its amortisation stays at zero.

The note, already reconciled

DISCLOSURE reconciles opening to closing NBV for each year and category: additions, disposals, amortisation, impairment and reversals.

Sensitivity on the judgement calls

Useful lives and the capitalisation rate are the assumptions auditors challenge. SENSITIVITY shows what moving each one does to the charge.

Frequently asked questions

Which categories does it cover?

Eight: software, patents, trademarks, licences, customer relationships, brand, R&D projects and goodwill. Each has its own useful life, residual value, additions, disposals and impairment trigger.

Is it monthly?

Yes. 60 monthly periods from the start date on INPUTS, with annual totals for the disclosure note and the dashboard.

What does it not do?

The category schedules amortise on a straight line; declining balance is available per asset on the register. Impairment here is a simplified annual test driven by a trigger percentage per category, not a cash-flow forecast. For a full CGU test with value in use, see the IAS 36 Impairment Model.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.
