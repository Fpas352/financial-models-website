Impairment Testing Model (IAS 36)

Test goodwill and other assets across eight cash-generating units: indicator assessment, a ten-year value in use DCF with a WACC build-up, fair value less costs to sell, the recoverable amount, the loss allocated goodwill first, and the headroom and sensitivity the disclosure needs.

The problem it solves

The annual goodwill test is often a DCF someone built years ago, with a discount rate nobody can rebuild and a sensitivity note written by hand. Auditors ask how far the WACC or the growth rate has to move before headroom disappears, and the model does not say.

This model calculates value in use and fair value less costs to sell for each CGU, takes the higher as recoverable amount, and shows the headroom with breakeven WACC, terminal growth and EBITDA margin beside it.

Who it's for

Technical accounting and group reporting teams running the annual goodwill test. Financial controllers at groups with acquired businesses. Valuation advisers and auditors who need a transparent benchmark for a client's test.

What's in the model

Every workbook opens on a COVER tab with instructions and a linked tab index. Then:

INPUTS

Reporting date, tax rate, risk-free rate, equity and debt risk premiums, long-term growth and cost to sell; then for each CGU the carrying amounts of goodwill, intangibles, PP&E and other assets, revenue, growth, margins, capex, working capital, beta, gearing, size and country premiums, and trading and transaction multiples. Base, Upside, Downside and Stress scenarios.

INDICATOR_ASSESSMENT

Sixteen external and internal indicators per CGU, from a fall in market value to operating losses, with a summary of whether a test is required.

GOODWILL_ALLOCATION

Goodwill by CGU, months since the last test, carrying amounts by asset type and a five-year goodwill movement schedule.

VIU_DCF

Ten-year free cash flow per CGU from revenue and margin, discounted at a WACC built from CAPM and the cost of debt, with a Gordon growth terminal value.

FVLCTS

Fair value from a trading multiple and a transaction multiple, weighted 60/40, less costs to sell, with sensitivity to the multiple.

RECOVERABLE_AMOUNT

The higher of VIU and FVLCTS, which one it was, headroom or impairment, and the loss amount.

IMPAIRMENT_CALC

The loss allocated to goodwill first, then pro rata across intangibles, PP&E and other assets, with post-impairment carrying amounts.

REVERSAL_TRACKING

Monthly monitoring of reversal indicators per CGU, capped at the carrying amount there would have been without the impairment, with goodwill reversal blocked.

SENSITIVITY

Headroom against WACC, terminal growth and EBITDA margin for each CGU, and a two-way grid of WACC against growth.

HEADROOM

Headroom, breakeven WACC, growth and margin with the cushion on each, a risk rating, and a five-year carrying amount projection.

DISCLOSURE

Key assumptions and sensitivity items under IAS 36.134, impairment losses under IAS 36.130, and the IAS 36.135 items, by CGU.

DASHBOARD

Carrying against recoverable amount, headroom by CGU, goodwill allocation, VIU composition and breakeven WACC against actual.

MONTHLY_TRACKING

Monthly P&L and free cash flow by CGU, with revenue against budget.

SCENARIO_COMPARISON

Key assumptions, WACC and an estimated VIU and headroom under all four scenarios side by side.

EXTENDED_SENS

A supplementary grid flexing reference values by a row shock and a column factor.

CHECKS

54 integrity checks, including recoverable amount equal to the higher of VIU and FVLCTS, and post-impairment carrying amounts reconciling for every CGU.

What makes it different

Both measures of recoverable amount

Value in use from a ten-year DCF and fair value less costs to sell from market multiples, calculated side by side for every CGU, with the basis shown.

A WACC you can rebuild

Cost of equity from the risk-free rate, beta, equity risk premium, size premium and country risk premium; cost of debt from the risk-free rate plus a credit spread; weighted by your gearing.

Allocation in the right order

A loss reduces goodwill first, then the other assets pro rata to their carrying amounts, with a check that post-impairment amounts reconcile.

Headroom with breakevens

For each CGU: how far WACC can rise, or terminal growth and EBITDA margin fall, before headroom reaches zero, with a LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH risk rating.

The disclosure items laid out

Key assumptions, headroom and the change in each assumption that would eliminate it, set out by CGU under the IAS 36.134 headings.

Frequently asked questions

How many CGUs can it test?

Eight. The demo uses banking segments, from retail and corporate banking to insurance and an international unit. Names and every assumption are inputs.

Does it handle reversals?

Yes, for assets other than goodwill. REVERSAL_TRACKING tests whether recoverable amount exceeds carrying amount and caps the reversal at the carrying amount there would have been without the impairment. Goodwill reversals are blocked.

What does it not do?

Value in use is discounted at a post-tax WACC used as a proxy for the pre-tax rate, and the projection runs ten years where IAS 36 expects five unless a longer period can be justified. Breakeven WACC, growth and margin on HEADROOM are closed-form approximations, not goal-seek results. Fair value is based on multiples.

Are there macros?

No. Open formulas, no VBA. Every number traces back to an input.

Can I see it before I buy?

Yes. The free sample is the complete workbook with every formula and check live, inputs locked, so you evaluate it on the demo company.
